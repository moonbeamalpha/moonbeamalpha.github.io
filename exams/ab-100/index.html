The exam

What is the AB-100 exam?

AB-100 is the Microsoft Agentic AI Business Solutions Architect credential — the senior-architect exam for designing, deploying, and governing agentic-first business solutions across the full Microsoft AI stack: Copilot Studio, Microsoft Foundry, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Dynamics 365 Copilot, and Power Platform AI. AB-100 covers planning AI-powered business solutions (ROI analysis, multi-agent strategy), designing agents and orchestration (task / autonomous / prompt-and-response agents, MCP extensibility, Foundry Tools), and deploying with full ALM, telemetry, responsible AI, and security.

AB-100 is breadth-heavy and architecture-first. It validates that you can analyse business requirements and recommend agentic patterns, decide build-vs-buy across Foundry / Copilot Studio / prebuilt agents, design multi-agent solutions using Agent2Agent (A2A) and Model Context Protocol (MCP), orchestrate Microsoft 365 Copilot for Sales / Service and Dynamics 365 AI, and define ALM, governance, and audit trails for agents and AI models. Expect scenario questions that hand you a multi-system enterprise architecture and ask you to recommend the right primitive, the right deployment slot, or the right control.

Common traps

Where AB-100 candidates slip

Five recurring misconceptions that trip up otherwise well-prepared AB-100 candidates, grounded in the current skills outline.

The right answer matches the stated constraint

AB-100 distractors are frequently correct answers — just to a different question. A design that's ideal for cost efficiency may be wrong when the scenario states a data-residency requirement, and a design that's ideal for governance may be wrong when the scenario prioritises time-to-market. Read for the specific constraint named in the stem before evaluating the options.

Smallest sufficient capability beats the most powerful one

As an architecture exam, AB-100 rewards restraint: a declarative Microsoft 365 Copilot extension often beats a custom-engine agent, a prebuilt Dynamics 365 Copilot feature often beats a bespoke build, and an AI Builder prompt often beats a fine-tuned model. If a scenario doesn't demand the heavier option's extra capability, it's usually the wrong answer.

Know which platform owns a capability by default

Copilot Studio, Microsoft Foundry, Microsoft 365 Copilot extensibility, and embedded Dynamics 365 Copilot all overlap in what they can technically do. Questions frequently ask which one is the primary, intended surface for a given task — extending an existing Copilot experience is a different answer than building a new agent from scratch, even when both are technically possible.

Responsible AI is the tie-breaker, not an afterthought

When two architecturally sound designs are otherwise equal, the exam expects you to pick the one that adds a responsible-AI control — an impact assessment, a content-safety classifier, an audit trail, or a refusal pattern. Treating responsible AI as a separate checklist item rather than a deciding factor is a common way to miss an otherwise straightforward question.

The scenario paragraph carries the answer

AB-100 items are typically a multi-paragraph business setup followed by one short decision. The constraint that determines the correct answer — a compliance requirement, an existing platform investment, a stated headcount limit — is usually buried in the setup, not repeated in the question line. Skimming straight to the question is the fastest way to pick a technically-correct-but-wrong answer.

10-week study plan

Suggested AB-100 study plan

Most candidates pass AB-100 after eight to twelve weeks of focused study, depending on hands-on experience with Copilot Studio, Microsoft Foundry, and Dynamics 365 Copilot. The ten-week plan below maps onto the three AB-100 domains (weighted toward Deploy at 40–45%), Azure Mastery's adaptive sessions, and the in-app exam simulator. Adjust pace to taste — the readiness gauge tells you when you're done, not the calendar.

  1. Plan AI-powered business solutions

    • Week 1: Requirements analysis — agent use cases for task automation, data analytics, and decision-making; grounding-data review (accuracy, relevance, timeliness); organising business data for AI consumption.
    • Week 2: Overall AI strategy — Cloud Adoption Framework for Azure (AI track), multi-agent solutions across Microsoft 365 Copilot / Copilot Studio / Microsoft Foundry, build-vs-buy-vs-extend, prompt-engineering guidelines, AI Center of Excellence.
    • Week 3: ROI analysis — total cost of ownership, build/buy/extend trade-offs, Dynamics 365 multi-app AI scenarios.

  2. Design AI-powered business solutions

    • Week 4: Design agents and AI — Dynamics 365 customer experience/service Copilot customisations, task / autonomous / prompt-and-response agents, Foundry Tools, topics with fallback in Copilot Studio.
    • Week 5: Extensibility — custom models in Foundry, agents in Microsoft 365 Copilot, MCP-based extensibility, Computer Use, agent behaviours including reasoning and voice mode.
    • Week 6: Orchestrate prebuilt agents — Dynamics 365 finance/supply chain, customer experience/service, Microsoft 365 Copilot for Sales / Service, Power Platform AI Hub, model routing.

  3. Deploy AI-powered business solutions

    • Week 7: Analyse, monitor, and tune — agent-monitoring tools, backlog and user-feedback analysis, telemetry interpretation, model-performance metrics.
    • Week 8: Testing — agent test strategy and metrics, validation criteria for custom AI models, end-to-end scenarios across multiple Dynamics 365 apps, prompt best-practices validation.
    • Week 9: ALM, responsible AI, security, governance — ALM for data / Copilot Studio agents / Foundry agents / custom AI models / Dynamics 365 AI; agent and model security; prompt-manipulation mitigations; responsible-AI review; data-residency compliance; access controls; audit trails.

  4. Sharpen, simulate, schedule

    • Week 10: Run Focus Weak Spots every morning, then two end-to-end Exam Simulator runs at full 100-minute length. Schedule the exam when readiness gauge is 740+ with reasonable confidence — AB-100 punishes a single weak domain.

Frequently asked

AB-100 FAQs

How much does the AB-100 exam cost?

The AB-100 voucher costs USD $165 in the United States (around £128 in the UK; pricing varies by region). As an Expert-tier exam, AB-100 is rarely included in free-voucher promotions — if one appears around Microsoft Build or Microsoft Ignite, it's worth grabbing. Renewal itself stays free.

Does the AB-100 certification expire?

Yes. Like Microsoft's other role-based certifications, AB-100 expires annually, with a free 25–30 question renewal assessment on Microsoft Learn available in the six-month window before expiry — scoped to whatever changed since you last certified (Foundry, Copilot Studio MCP features, Dynamics 365 orchestration, and similar). AB-900, the Fundamentals-tier exam, doesn't expire.

What is the AB-100 retake policy if I fail?

24 hours before your first retake, then 14 days for the second and third, capped at five attempts in any rolling 12 months with a fresh voucher each time. Given how broad AB-100's syllabus is, treat a fail as a signal to target the weakest domain specifically rather than rebooking immediately.

What does the AB-100 exam format look like?

Like most Microsoft Certification exams, AB-100 has 40–60 questions in a mix of formats, with the exact timing confirmed at registration. It runs across a 100-minute session (120 minutes with seat time for admin), online or at a test centre, and scored out of 1000 with a 700 pass mark. In Azure Mastery's AB-100 bank you'll practise a heavier share of multi-paragraph scenario questions than the associate-level agent exam banks, so you spend real time reading a business constraint before picking the architecture that fits it.

How long should I study for AB-100?

Eight to twelve weeks if you already have hands-on time with at least one of Copilot Studio, Microsoft Foundry, or Dynamics 365 Copilot. Give it three to four months if multi-agent orchestration is genuinely new — AB-100 expects you to design end-to-end solutions, not just describe the pieces.

AB-100 vs AB-620 — which agent cert should I take?

Different altitudes. AB-620 certifies the maker who builds an agent in Copilot Studio. AB-100 certifies the architect who decides which platform an agent belongs on, how it's governed, and how the ALM and ROI case gets made across Copilot Studio, Foundry, and Dynamics 365 at once. Build the agent first; architect the estate second.

AB-100 vs AI-103 vs AB-620 — how do the roles differ?

AI-103 is the code-first developer path (Python, Foundry). AB-620 is the Copilot Studio builder path (low-code agents). AB-100 sits above both as the architecture credential spanning every Microsoft AI surface. Get comfortable with an implementation credential before attempting the architecture-level exam.

Do I need prerequisites before attempting AB-100?

Not formally, but AB-100 assumes it. Microsoft expects candidates to already hold AI-901-level knowledge, plus working experience with at least one of AI-102, AI-103, PL-200, PL-300, PL-400, PL-500, or AB-620. AB-100 tests architectural decisions across platforms you're assumed to already know individually — it isn't where most candidates start their Microsoft AI journey.
