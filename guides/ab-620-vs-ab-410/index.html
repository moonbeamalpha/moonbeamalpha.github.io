AB-620 vs AB-410: agent builder or intelligent-app builder?

Both are new-in-2026 Copilot-adjacent Associate exams. See how they split the work, then pick the one that matches what you actually build.

Updated 4 September 2026 · ~9 min read

Quick answer

AB-620 or AB-410: the short version

Both exams sit inside the same Copilot Studio ecosystem, but they certify two different jobs. Which one you sit first should follow the work in front of you, not a ranking of which sounds more advanced.

Choose AB-620 if you build the agent itself

AB-620 (Microsoft Certified: AI Agent Builder Associate) is the right exam if your job is designing and extending custom agents inside Copilot Studio: conversational topics, generative-answer configuration, knowledge-source grounding, tool integrations through MCP or custom connectors, and multi-agent orchestration with the Agent2Agent protocol. You are the person who decides how the agent reasons and responds.

Choose AB-410 if you build the application around it

AB-410 (Microsoft Certified: Intelligent Applications Builder Associate) is the right exam if your job is building the Power Platform application, data model, and automation that consumes agents, prompts, and AI models: Dataverse tables, model-driven and canvas apps, generative pages, cloud flows, and AI Hub prompts. You are the person who decides how the business actually uses what the agent produces.

Neither is a formal prerequisite for the other. See "Do you need one before the other?" below for how the two certifications fit together when you own the whole solution.

What AB-620 actually tests

What AB-620 actually tests

AB-620 is scenario-driven and Copilot Studio-specific. Expect a configuration screen or a described business constraint, and a question about what to change or which component to use.

AB-620 skills measured (outline effective 21 April 2026)
DomainWeight
Plan & configure agent solutions30–35%
Integrate & extend agents40–45%
Test & manage agents20–25%

Planning covers integration with enterprise systems, identity strategy, channel and deployment planning, and responsible AI, then moves into building agent flows and configuring topics: trigger phrases, tools, adaptive cards, and variables. The largest domain, integrate and extend, covers connecting to enterprise knowledge sources such as Azure AI Search and Power Platform connectors, adding tools including MCP and REST APIs, and — the section most candidates underestimate — multi-agent collaboration: wiring a Foundry agent or a Fabric data agent into an orchestrator, and building cross-agent solutions with the Agent2Agent protocol. Test and manage covers evaluation methods and application lifecycle management: solutions, environment variables, and Power Platform Pipelines rather than manual copy-paste between environments.

What AB-410 actually tests

What AB-410 actually tests

AB-410 is task-based and spans two different app-building paradigms plus a heavyweight automation domain, so it rewards candidates who are comfortable moving between Dataverse, both app types, and Power Fx in the same session.

AB-410 skills measured (outline effective 15 May 2026)
DomainWeight
Create a foundation for intelligent applications25–30%
Create intelligent applications25–30%
Build business application logic & automation40–45%

The foundation domain covers Dataverse table and column design, relationships, prompt columns, security, and choosing the right AI-enabled build approach and environment type before you start. Create intelligent applications splits between model-driven apps — forms, views, generative pages built from natural language, and dashboards — and canvas apps, where accessibility, reusable components, and error handling matter as much as layout. The largest domain, business logic and automation, is where AB-410 differs most from a plain app-building exam: cloud flow triggers and connectors, AI Hub prompts consumed from both apps and flows, and the distinctions between business rules, business process flows, and calculated, rollup, or formula columns that recur throughout the exam.

Prerequisite question

Do you need one before the other?

No. AB-620 and AB-410 are both standalone Associate certifications, and Microsoft does not require either one as a prerequisite for the other. You can sit AB-620 without ever touching AB-410's Dataverse and app-building content, and you can sit AB-410 without ever building an agent from scratch in Copilot Studio.

Where the two genuinely connect is in what they build together. AB-620 certifies the agent's internal logic; AB-410 certifies the application, data model, and automation that surrounds it. A single Copilot solution — say, an internal support assistant with a Dataverse-backed case log, a canvas app for agents to review flagged conversations, and an approval flow for escalations — draws on both skill sets at once. If you own that kind of end-to-end solution, sitting both certifications maps directly onto your actual job; if you own only one half, sit the one that matches it and treat the other as optional.

If you are deciding purely on order, sit whichever one reflects your stronger existing skill first: candidates who already build in Copilot Studio daily tend to find AB-620 the faster route in, while candidates who already model Dataverse tables and build canvas apps tend to find AB-410 faster. Sitting the exam closer to your current work first also means the second exam's shared Power Platform foundation — solutions, environments, and ALM basics — needs less relearning.

Career value

How each certification helps your career

AB-620 is the credential to point to when a role description says "Copilot Studio developer", "AI agent builder", or asks specifically for agent orchestration, MCP tooling, or multi-agent design. It signals that you can be trusted with the parts of a Copilot solution that are hardest to get wrong: groundedness, tool access, and how agents hand off to one another.

AB-410 is the credential for "Power Platform developer" or "low-code application builder" roles that now expect AI literacy as standard, not as a bonus skill. It is also the direct successor path for anyone who previously built toward PL-200 (Power Platform Functional Consultant Associate) — the PL-200T00 training course has been replaced by AB-410T00, and prior PL-200 experience with Dataverse and canvas apps transfers cleanly into AB-410's foundation and application domains.

Neither certification guarantees a role or a pay rise on its own. What both reliably provide is a structured way to demonstrate a specific, in-demand skill set to a hiring manager or an internal promotion panel, backed by a Microsoft-set bar rather than a self-assessment.

Take both?

Should you take both AB-620 and AB-410?

For anyone who owns a Copilot solution end to end — the agent, the app around it, and the data model underneath — taking both is the strongest option on the table. Microsoft's own certification pathing groups the two together for exactly this reason: one exam proves you can build the agent, the other proves you can build everything the agent depends on and everything that depends on the agent.

For a specialist who only ever touches one side of that boundary, taking both is optional. A pure Copilot Studio developer gets little additional signal from AB-410's Dataverse and canvas-app content, and a pure Power Platform app builder who never designs agent logic gets little additional signal from AB-620's orchestration-heavy domains. In those cases, sit the one that matches the job and revisit the other only if the role expands.

If you do plan to sit both, studying them in sequence rather than in parallel makes the shared Power Platform foundation work in your favour instead of doubling your workload. The AB-620 study guide lays out a four-week plan you can use as the first half of a combined project.

Frequently asked

AB-620 vs AB-410 FAQs

Do I need AB-620 before AB-410, or the other way round?

Neither. AB-620 and AB-410 are both standalone Associate exams with no formal prerequisite between them, and Microsoft's booking system will not check which one you hold before letting you schedule either. They are designed to be taken in whichever order matches the work you actually do.

What's the real difference between AB-620 and AB-410?

AB-620 tests whether you can design and extend the agent itself inside Copilot Studio: topics, generative orchestration, knowledge sources, tools, and multi-agent protocols. AB-410 tests whether you can build the Power Platform application, data model, and automation around an agent: Dataverse tables, model-driven and canvas apps, cloud flows, and AI Hub prompts. One is agent-layer, the other is application-layer. AB-410 stays low-code throughout — Power Apps designers, Dataverse, and cloud flow triggers, with no code editor required. AB-620 leans more code-first, since integrating agents with MCP tool definitions, custom connector schemas, and the Agent2Agent protocol involves reading and writing structured configuration that Copilot Studio's canvas doesn't fully abstract away.

Is AB-410 the same as the old PL-200?

No, but it is the successor path for makers who previously targeted PL-200 (Power Platform Functional Consultant Associate). The PL-200T00 training course was replaced by AB-410T00, and AB-410 folds in the AI Hub, generative-page, and Copilot-integration content that PL-200 never covered. Prior PL-200 experience with Dataverse and Power Apps is a genuine head start, but it does not cover the AI Hub prompt or generative-page objectives on its own.

Which is harder, AB-620 or AB-410?

They are comparably demanding but hard in different ways. AB-620 concentrates difficulty in its Integrate & Extend domain, worth 40 to 45%, where multi-agent orchestration, MCP tools, and the Agent2Agent protocol require precise distinctions. AB-410 spreads difficulty across two application types (model-driven and canvas) plus a 40 to 45% automation domain covering cloud flows, AI Hub prompts, and business logic, so it rewards breadth over depth in any single area.

Do AB-620 and AB-410 expire the same way?

Yes. Both are Microsoft Associate certifications, and both require annual renewal through a free, unproctored online assessment on Microsoft Learn during the six-month window before expiry. Passing one does not extend or affect the renewal clock on the other; each certification renews independently.

Can I take AB-620 and AB-410 close together?

Yes. There is no cooling-off period between different exam codes, only between repeat attempts at the same one. Because both exams assume familiarity with the Power Platform environment model, solutions, and ALM basics, studying them back to back means the second exam's foundation section moves faster than the first one did.
