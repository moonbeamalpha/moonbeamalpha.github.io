The exam

What is the AI-300 exam?

AI-300 carries the credential name Operationalizing Machine Learning and Generative AI Solutions — the exam behind postings for "MLOps Engineer", "GenAIOps Engineer", "AI Platform Operations", or "ML Reliability Engineer" roles. It groups AIOps into two halves: the machine-learning side (Azure Machine Learning, model lifecycle) and the generative-AI side (Microsoft Foundry, agents, GenAIOps), which is why the outline reads as two exams welded together rather than one narrow specialism.

AI-300 is hands-on and lifecycle-focused. It validates that you can design and implement an MLOps infrastructure (workspace and asset management, infrastructure as code), implement machine learning model lifecycle and operations (training orchestration, registration, deployment, monitoring), design and implement a GenAIOps infrastructure (Foundry environments, foundation-model deployment, prompt versioning), implement generative AI quality assurance and observability (evaluation, safety checks, tracing), and optimise generative AI systems and model performance (RAG tuning, fine-tuning). Expect scenario questions that span Bicep/CLI deployment snippets, monitoring dashboards, and trade-off reasoning between fine-tuning and retrieval.

This is the largest domain, and it's the working day of an MLOps engineer. Training questions cover experiment tracking with MLflow, automated machine learning, notebook-based exploration, hyperparameter tuning, distributed training for large models, and comparing performance across jobs. Where the ML lifecycle domain is about traditional models, this one is about the infrastructure a generative AI or agentic application runs on in Microsoft Foundry. Quality assurance here means building the evaluation harness before an application ships, not reacting after it fails. Candidates create test datasets and data mappings for comprehensive evaluation, implement AI quality metrics such as groundedness, relevance, coherence, and fluency, configure risk and safety evaluations for harmful content, and set up automated evaluation workflows using built-in and custom metrics. Observability picks up once the system is live: examining continuous monitoring in Foundry, tracking latency, throughput, and response-time metrics, watching token consumption and cost, and configuring the logging, tracing, and debugging that make a production incident diagnosable rather than a mystery.

The smallest domain by weight, and a dense one. Retrieval-augmented generation questions test tuning similarity thresholds, chunk sizes, and retrieval strategies, choosing and fine-tuning embedding models for a domain, combining semantic and keyword-based hybrid search, and proving an improvement with relevance metrics or A/B testing rather than instinct. Fine-tuning questions cover designing advanced fine-tuning methods, creating and managing synthetic data for that fine-tuning, monitoring a fine-tuned model's performance, and managing it from development through production. Expect the exam to reward knowing when tuning retrieval beats fine-tuning the model, and when neither beats simply picking a better base model.

Common traps

Where AI-300 candidates slip

Registering an MLflow model isn't the same as deploying one

Candidates conflate "the model is registered" with "the model is production-ready." Registration captures the artifact, its lineage, and a responsible-AI evaluation; deployment is a separate step that chooses a real-time or batch endpoint and a rollout strategy. Items that describe a model sitting in the registry with no traffic are testing whether you notice deployment hasn't happened yet.

Data drift and prompt drift are monitored by different mechanisms

Traditional model monitoring watches feature distributions and prediction drift against a training baseline. A generative or agentic system doesn't have that baseline — its "drift" shows up as evaluation-metric decay on groundedness, relevance, or safety scores over time. Reaching for classic drift monitoring on a GenAIOps workload, or vice versa, is a common wrong-answer pattern.

A better embedding model doesn't fix a query the retriever never saw

When retrieval misses part of a compound question, the instinct is to tune the embedding model or the chunk size. Neither helps if the original query never expressed every condition being asked about — that's a query-construction problem, not a scoring problem, and the exam expects you to recognise which one you're looking at before picking a fix.

Provisioned throughput solves capacity, not cost

Provisioned throughput units guarantee latency and capacity for high-volume foundation-model workloads, but they are billed regardless of usage. Items describing a spiky, low-volume workload with a cost constraint are testing whether you'll wrongly reach for PTUs when a serverless, pay-per-token endpoint fits the actual traffic pattern.

Network restrictions can block the pipeline they're meant to protect

Locking a workspace or Foundry project behind a private endpoint is correct security practice, but a CI/CD pipeline, a registry share across workspaces, or a monitoring integration can silently fail once that boundary goes up unless the corresponding rule or managed identity is granted through. The exam rewards spotting the missing rule, not just praising the lockdown.

Suggested AI-300 study plan

Most candidates pass AI-300 after four to eight weeks of focused study, depending on prior Azure experience. The six-week plan below maps onto the five AI-300 domains, Azure Mastery's adaptive sessions, and the in-app exam simulator. Adjust pace to taste — the readiness gauge tells you when you're done, not the calendar.

  1. MLOps infrastructure and lifecycle

    • Week 1: Design and implement an MLOps infrastructure — CI/CD for ML, environment management (Conda, Docker), feature stores, model registries, governance, reproducibility, lineage.
    • Week 2: Implement machine learning model lifecycle and operations (largest domain, 25–30%) — training pipelines, AutoML, deployment to managed online and batch endpoints, A/B traffic, drift monitoring, retraining triggers.

  2. GenAIOps and quality assurance

    • Week 3: Design and implement a GenAIOps infrastructure — prompt versioning and registries, evaluation pipelines, deployment patterns for LLM apps, feature-flag prompt rollouts, observability for tokens and cost.
    • Week 4: Implement generative AI quality assurance and observability — groundedness checks, hallucination detection, content-safety telemetry, drift on prompts/RAG, distributed tracing.

  3. Optimisation, sharpen, simulate

    • Week 5: Optimize generative AI systems and model performance — semantic and embedding caches, batching, model-selection trade-offs (small vs large), fine-tuning vs few-shot vs RAG, token budget management, capacity planning.
    • Week 6: Run Focus Weak Spots every morning, then two end-to-end Exam Simulator runs at full 100-minute length. Schedule the exam when readiness gauge is 750+ with reasonable confidence.

Frequently asked

AI-300 FAQs

AI-300 vs AI-200 — which next?

AI-200 first. AI-200 (AI Cloud Developer Associate) builds the cloud platform that AI workloads run on — compute, vector databases, integration, security. AI-300 (ML Operations Engineer Associate) operates ML and generative-AI systems on that platform. AI-200 is upstream of AI-300 in most roles.

Did AI-300 replace DP-100?

Yes. Microsoft retired DP-100 and the Azure Data Scientist Associate certification on 1 June 2026 and identifies AI-300, Machine Learning Operations Engineer Associate, as its replacement. The emphasis changed: DP-100 focused on data-science model development, while AI-300 focuses on operationalizing machine learning and generative AI through MLOps, GenAIOps, observability, quality, and lifecycle automation.

Are there prerequisites for AI-300?

Microsoft sets no mandatory prerequisite exam for AI-300. Its published audience profile expects subject-matter expertise in MLOps and GenAIOps on Azure, a data-science background with Python experience, and an entry-level understanding of DevOps practices such as GitHub Actions and command-line tooling. Candidates without hands-on Azure Machine Learning or Foundry experience typically need longer to prepare, even though nothing blocks booking the exam directly.

What format is the AI-300 exam?

Microsoft's general guidance for AI-300, in common with most Associate exams, is 40–60 questions with formats that vary by sitting. In Azure Mastery's AI-300 bank, single-answer and scenario-based questions carry most of the weight, alongside drag-to-match, dropdown-select, and hot-area items and a run of linked case studies — original practice content, not a copy of Microsoft's live item mix. Timed practice sessions use the app's own session clock and supported interactive item types; adaptive study and Focus Weak Spots then target the domains where your accuracy is lowest.

Is AI-300 a permanent credential, or does it need renewing?

It needs renewing. Microsoft Associate certifications including AI-300 expire annually. Renewal is free — a 25–30 question online assessment on Microsoft Learn within the six-month window before your expiration date. The renewal targets recent skills outline updates, so staying current is straightforward if you remain broadly active in the role. (Fundamentals certifications such as AZ-900 are different — those don't expire.)

What does the AI-300 exam voucher cost?

The AI-300 voucher is USD $165 in the United States. Pricing varies by region — in the UK it's typically around £128. Microsoft sometimes runs free-voucher promotions during events such as Microsoft Build or Microsoft Ignite, so check your Microsoft Learn profile for any active offers before booking. AI-300 also requires annual renewal (free, online), so factor that into long-term cost planning.

How many weeks of prep does AI-300 typically take?

Allow four to eight weeks of focused study if you already work with Azure Machine Learning or generative AI systems. Spend longer if MLOps, GenAIOps, deployment infrastructure, observability, quality evaluation, or model lifecycle automation are new to you. Azure Mastery's readiness gauge helps you decide when to schedule the exam.

How soon can I resit AI-300 after a fail?

The first retake is allowed after 24 hours. Second and third retakes each require a 14-day wait. Microsoft caps retakes at five attempts per 12-month rolling period. Each attempt requires a new voucher purchase.

Can I revise for AI-300 without a connection?

Yes. Question practice, scoring, and the readiness prediction all run on-device, so you can study on a flight or a commute with no connection. No account is required, and your study data stays on your device with optional private iCloud sync.
