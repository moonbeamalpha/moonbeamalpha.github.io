The exam

What is the SC-100 exam?

SC-100 is the exam for the Microsoft Certified: Cybersecurity Architect Expert credential — the most senior security cert in Microsoft's track. It's the credential hiring managers expect for "Cybersecurity Architect", "Lead Security Engineer", or "Cloud Security Architect" roles.

Passing SC-100 alone doesn't award the certification: Microsoft also requires one of three active Associate-level credentials — Identity and Access Administrator Associate (SC-300), Security Operations Analyst Associate (SC-200), or Cloud and AI Security Engineer Associate (SC-500).

Microsoft replaced the retired AZ-500 route with SC-500 in the current prerequisite list. You can take SC-100 before earning the Associate credential, but the expert certification is awarded only after both requirements are active on your profile. The prerequisite is the active Associate credential, not merely a pass on its exam.

SC-100 is design-oriented and trade-off-heavy. Unlike Associate exams that test individual-service knowledge, it tests architectural reasoning: picking the right strategy under real-world constraints. It validates that you can:

  • Apply the Microsoft Cybersecurity Reference Architecture (MCRA)
  • Design a Zero Trust strategy and a ransomware-resilience stance
  • Build SecOps strategies around Microsoft Sentinel and Defender XDR
  • Design identity strategies in Microsoft Entra — Conditional Access, PIM, Identity Governance
  • Design infrastructure security spanning endpoints, servers, containers, networks, and keys
  • Design application and data security, including DevSecOps

Expect scenario questions where multiple answers could work; the right one is whichever best satisfies the stated constraints.

Common traps

Where SC-100 candidates slip

Five recurring misconceptions that trip up otherwise well-prepared SC-100 candidates, grounded in the current skills outline.

Zero Trust is an architecture, not a single control

Candidates who deployed MFA or network segmentation sometimes treat either one alone as doing Zero Trust.

  • A single strong control, deployed harder, still reads as incomplete if the other pillars stay silent.
  • Build a Zero Trust answer that touches at least two pillars before you commit to a single-control fix.

MCRA, MCSB, and CAF or WAF answer different questions

Candidates reach for whichever framework name they remember best rather than the one the scenario actually calls for.

  • MCRA is a reference architecture; MCSB provides prescriptive security recommendations; CAF and WAF guide adoption and workload design.
  • A posture-management tool can assess an environment against MCSB, but the benchmark itself does not perform the measurement.

The enterprise access model has replaced legacy admin tiering

Candidates who studied older AD-centric tiering default to Tier 0, 1, 2 language on a privileged-access scenario.

  • Reasoning in terms of the control, management, and data-or-workload planes now replaces tier numbers entirely.
  • Retire Tier 0/1/2 language from your answers and reason in terms of the three enterprise access model planes.

Posture management and active response are different domains

Defender for Cloud appearing in a scenario doesn't automatically make the question about security operations.

  • A SOAR or incident-response design belongs in security operations, whatever product name the scenario mentions.
  • Ask whether the scenario wants a measurement of risk or a response to an active threat before choosing a domain.

The prerequisite gates the certification, not the exam booking

Candidates researching "can I take SC-100 first" often assume the prerequisite blocks registration itself.

  • Content readiness is what SC-100 grades; the prerequisite sits outside the exam, as a separate certification-award policy.
  • Book SC-100 whenever you're ready on content, and separately earn an active SC-200, SC-300, or SC-500 prerequisite certification.

6-week study plan

Suggested SC-100 study plan

Practise security architecture decisions across strategy, infrastructure, applications and data. Use these six weeks of study blocks to build your trade-off reasoning, then rehearse.

  1. Security architecture frameworks

    • Review the Microsoft Cybersecurity Reference Architecture.
    • Explore the Microsoft Cloud Security Benchmark.
    • Review the Cloud Adoption Framework security baseline.

  2. Zero Trust and resilience

    • Design Zero Trust across identities, endpoints and networks.
    • Extend the strategy to applications and data.
    • Review ransomware resilience and recovery.

  3. Compliance and risk

    • Review compliance requirements and their design implications.
    • Consider sovereign-cloud constraints.
    • Compare risk-assessment approaches.

  4. Security operations strategy

    • Design SecOps with Sentinel and Defender XDR.
    • Plan Entra access controls and identity governance.
    • Review Purview compliance strategy.

  5. Endpoint and server strategy

    • Compare Defender for Endpoint and Defender for Servers.
    • Review secure baselines.
    • Plan hybrid and multi-cloud coverage with Azure Arc.

  6. Network security strategy

    • Design segmentation and perimeter protection.
    • Plan private connectivity.
    • Review Defender for Cloud network maps.

  7. Keys and secure access

    • Compare Key Vault, customer-managed keys and HSMs.
    • Plan secret management.
    • Review Bastion and just-in-time VM access.

  8. Container security

    • Review Defender for Containers and AKS hardening.
    • Plan image scanning.
    • Consider software supply-chain protection.

  9. Application security strategy

    • Review secure development lifecycle choices.
    • Plan DevSecOps and supply-chain security.
    • Practise threat modelling.

  10. Data security strategy

    • Review Purview Information Protection and labels.
    • Compare encryption choices for SQL, Cosmos DB and Storage.
    • Consider data residency.

  11. Practise trade-off reasoning

    • Use Focus Weak Spots to revisit gaps.
    • Re-read scenarios you missed.
    • Explain why each alternative is unsuitable.

  12. Timed rehearsal

    • Complete two 120-minute Exam Simulator sessions.
    • Review the reasoning behind missed answers.
    • Plan further practice where needed.

Frequently asked

SC-100 FAQs

MCRA vs MCSB — which framework does a given SC-100 scenario want?

MCRA shows how security capabilities fit together, while MCSB provides prescriptive security recommendations. A posture-management tool can assess an environment against MCSB, but the benchmark itself is guidance rather than a measurement service.

What's the SC-100 prerequisite?

You can sit SC-100 with no prior certification. To earn the Cybersecurity Architect Expert credential, you must also hold an active SC-200, SC-300, or SC-500 Associate certification. Microsoft replaced the retired AZ-500 route with SC-500 in the current prerequisite list. An exam pass alone is not the prerequisite; the Associate credential must be active on your profile.

What format does the SC-100 exam use?

40–60 questions, with formats that vary by sitting. Azure Mastery's bank runs a similar spread: mostly single-answer, a meaningful share of scenario questions, yes/no and multi-select in roughly equal measure, plus a smaller mix of drag-to-match, dropdown, hotspot, ordering, and case-study items.

What's the SC-100 exam fee, and does renewal add to the cost?

The voucher costs USD $165 in the United States, around £128 in the UK. Microsoft occasionally runs free-voucher promotions around events like Build or Ignite. Renewal itself is free, a short annual online assessment, so factor only the original voucher into long-term cost.

Is SC-100 a lifetime certification, or does it need renewing?

The Cybersecurity Architect Expert credential is valid for one year. In the final six months, renew it at no charge through Microsoft's current online assessment; its length can change as the skills outline evolves. Fundamentals credentials such as AZ-900 do not expire.

SC-100 vs AZ-305 — which next for an architect?

Different lenses. SC-100 is the security architect cert: Zero Trust, ransomware resilience, SecOps strategy, identity governance. AZ-305 is the solutions architect cert: data, infrastructure, business continuity, identity and monitoring. They overlap on identity and governance; many senior architects hold both.

How soon can I resit SC-100 after a fail?

A first retake is available after 24 hours.

  • First retake: 24-hour wait.
  • Second and third retakes: 14-day wait each.
  • Five attempts maximum per rolling 12 months, a fresh voucher required each sitting.
How is SC-100 different from an Associate-level security exam?

Associate exams like SC-200 and SC-300 test configuring a named product correctly. SC-100 tests designing the strategy those products serve, choosing between Sentinel automation and Defender XDR-native response for stated constraints. Multiple options can be valid; only one best satisfies the scenario.
