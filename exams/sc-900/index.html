The exam

What is the SC-900 exam?

SC-900 is the Microsoft Certified: Security, Compliance, and Identity Fundamentals credential — the entry-level exam for anyone working with (or making decisions about) Microsoft's security stack across both Azure and Microsoft 365. It's pitched at business stakeholders, new and existing IT professionals, and students who want a credible foundation in Microsoft SCI solutions.

Common audiences include security analysts about to step into SC-200, identity administrators heading toward SC-300, and architects on the path to SC-100 (Cybersecurity Architect Expert).

The exam doesn't ask you to configure a Conditional Access policy or write a KQL query. It expects you to recognise scenarios across the SCI surface:

  • The shared responsibility model, defence-in-depth, and Zero Trust
  • Encryption vs hashing, and identity vs authentication vs authorization
  • The role of Microsoft Entra ID and its identity types
  • The capabilities of Microsoft Defender products and Microsoft Sentinel
  • Microsoft's compliance solutions, including Microsoft Purview

Common traps

Where SC-900 candidates slip

Five recurring misconceptions that trip up otherwise well-prepared SC-900 candidates, grounded in the current skills outline.

Authentication and authorization answer two different questions

Candidates use the two terms almost interchangeably in conversation, then mix them up the moment the exam tests them.

  • MFA and passwordless methods authenticate identity; RBAC authorises actions. Conditional Access is the policy engine that evaluates signals and can require stronger authentication or block access.
  • Ask whether the scenario is proving identity, assigning permissions, or evaluating access conditions before choosing a feature.

Defender is a family of products, not one product

SC-900 expects you to match a described surface to the specific Defender product that covers it, and the brand name alone will not get you there.

  • Email, devices, SaaS apps, and on-premises AD signals each map to a different named Defender product.
  • Name the exact surface in the scenario, such as email or endpoint, before choosing which Defender product answers it.

Microsoft Sentinel and Defender XDR cover different scopes

Both correlate security signal, so candidates treat Sentinel as just another Defender product rather than a separate platform.

  • Sentinel is a broad SIEM and automation platform for Microsoft and non-Microsoft data. Defender XDR correlates incidents across Defender products and integrates with Sentinel.
  • Pick Sentinel when the scenario centres on cross-environment log ingestion, analytics rules, or playbooks; pick Defender XDR for unified investigation across Defender workloads.

Sensitivity labels and retention labels solve different problems in Purview

Both live inside Microsoft Purview, so candidates assume one label type covers both classification and retention.

  • Sensitivity labels protect content based on what it is; retention labels govern how long that content is kept.
  • Pick retention over sensitivity whenever the scenario is about a legally required keep-or-delete period.

Compliance score is an output, not a separate tool

Candidates describe compliance score as its own product rather than a number that Compliance Manager calculates.

  • Compliance score is the weighted output Compliance Manager produces from whichever assessments you've chosen to run.
  • Remember compliance score reports on Compliance Manager's controls; it isn't a tool you configure on its own.

Frequently asked

SC-900 FAQs

Which Defender product owns which surface — does SC-900 test that?

Yes, repeatedly. SC-900 expects you to place Defender for Endpoint, Cloud Apps, Identity, Office 365, and Cloud against the surface each one actually protects rather than by product name alone. Answer Coach names the specific surface a wrong pick would have left uncovered.

SC-900 vs AZ-900 — which should I take first?

Pick based on which vocabulary you need first. SC-900 teaches Microsoft's security, identity, and compliance stack; AZ-900 teaches general Azure fundamentals like regions, compute, and pricing. Security-adjacent roles start with SC-900; newcomers to Azure generally start with AZ-900. Many candidates sit both.

What question formats come up on SC-900?

SC-900's assessed time is 45 minutes. Microsoft does not promise an exact format in advance, but says most Fundamentals exams contain 35–50 questions. Azure Master's original security-fundamentals bank uses single-select, scenario, multi-select, drag-to-match, hot-area, dropdown, case-study, and drag-and-drop practice.

What's the SC-900 exam fee, and does it vary by region?

The SC-900 voucher costs USD $99 in the United States, and pricing varies by region, around £77 in the UK. Microsoft occasionally runs free-voucher promotions around events like Build or Ignite, so check your Learn profile before booking.

Once passed, does an SC-900 certification lapse?

No. Microsoft Fundamentals certifications, including AZ-900, DP-900, AI-901, SC-900, and PL-900, never expire once you pass. Associate and Expert certifications such as SC-200 or SC-300 differ: those need an annual free renewal assessment on Microsoft Learn.

Purview compliance solutions — what's the recurring SC-900 trap?

Candidates reach for the Purview tool they've heard of, not the one built for the stated requirement, confusing a DLP policy with a sensitivity label, or a retention policy with an eDiscovery case. Answer Coach spells out that distinction whenever you miss one.

I need to resit SC-900 — how long is the wait?

The wait before your first retake is 24 hours.

  • First retake: 24-hour wait.
  • Second and third retakes: 14-day wait each.
  • Five attempts maximum per rolling 12 months, each needing a new voucher.
Is SC-900 worth taking if I'm not technical?

Yes. SC-900 targets a broad audience on purpose: business stakeholders weighing security procurement, IT generalists working near security, and students entering the field all sit it. You won't configure Conditional Access or write KQL, just pick which Defender product covers a described surface.
