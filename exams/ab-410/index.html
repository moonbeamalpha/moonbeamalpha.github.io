The exam

What is the AB-410 exam?

AB-410 is the Microsoft Intelligent Applications Builder credential — the role-based exam for makers who build AI-powered business solutions in Microsoft Power Platform using Copilot, natural-language prompts and low-code tools. AB-410 covers laying the foundation (solution design, environments, ALM and the Dataverse data model), creating the applications themselves (model-driven and canvas apps, including generative pages built from a written description), and building the logic and automation behind them (cloud flows, AI Hub prompts and AI Builder models, business rules and process flows). It is the successor route for makers who previously targeted PL-200 — Microsoft replaced the PL-200T00 course with AB-410T00.

AB-410 is hands-on and maker-shaped. It validates that you can turn stated requirements into the right component — a model-driven app, a canvas app, a cloud flow, a Power Pages site or an agent — then model the data in Dataverse, compose the app, and wire up the logic. Expect scenario questions that hand you a requirement and a constraint (must hold on every write path, must not block the save, must reach a service outside Dataverse) and ask which mechanism fits. Power Fx, prompt columns, generative pages, and the difference between calculated, rollup and formula columns all recur.

Common traps

Where AB-410 candidates slip

A business rule, a cloud flow, and a plug-in are not interchangeable

AB-410 stems often describe logic that has to run every time a record is saved, and candidates default to a cloud flow because that's the tool they reach for most in real work. A business rule fires synchronously on the write path and can block the save; a cloud flow runs after the fact and can't stop it. If the requirement says the record must never be saved in an invalid state, a flow is the wrong answer even when it would eventually run correctly.

Calculated, rollup, and formula columns update on different schedules

All three columns look identical on a form, which is exactly the trap. A calculated column evaluates when the record is retrieved, a rollup column runs on a schedule or a manual recalculation, and a formula column runs Power Fx on read like a calculated column but supports more logic. A scenario asking for a live aggregate across related child records that must always be current rules the rollup column out immediately.

A named formula is not the same thing as a global variable

Canvas-app questions test whether you reach for App.OnStart out of habit. A variable set there only holds a current value if OnStart actually ran — reload the app a different way and it's stale. A named formula recalculates automatically whenever its inputs change, with nothing to remember to trigger. Anywhere a value should just always be right, the formula is the safer design, and the exam rewards knowing that.

Model-driven, canvas, and Power Pages solve different distribution problems

A scenario naming an audience without a Dataverse licence or a tenant identity is describing Power Pages, not a canvas app with guest access bolted on. Model-driven suits many related tables and generated navigation; canvas suits a bespoke, pixel-level layout for a smaller task. Candidates who treat all three as "a way to show Dataverse data" misread the requirement before they even reach the component list.

6-week study plan

Suggested AB-410 study plan

Most candidates prepare for AB-410 in four to eight weeks, depending on how much Power Platform and Dataverse experience they already have. The six-week plan below maps onto the three AB-410 domains, Azure Mastery's adaptive sessions, and the in-app exam simulator. Adjust the pace to your background — the readiness gauge tells you when you're done, not the calendar.

  1. Plan and configure

    • Week 1: Create a foundation — analyse requirements to pick the right component, evaluate built-in agents, recommend extensibility options and environment types, and apply a solution and ALM strategy.
    • Week 2: Build the data model — tables in the data workspace, columns and data types, relationships, prompt columns, row summaries, public views, main forms and Dataverse security.

  2. Integrate and extend

    • Week 3: Create intelligent applications — model-driven forms and views, generative pages built from a written description, app composition and the site map, charts and dashboards.
    • Week 4: Canvas apps — data sources, accessibility and performance, reusable components and component libraries, named formulas and user-defined functions, variables and collections, error handling, testing with Monitor.

  3. Test, manage, sharpen, simulate

    • Week 5: Build business logic and automation (largest domain, 40–45%) — cloud flow triggers, connectors, approvals, actions, conditions and loops; AI Hub prompts and AI Builder models; business rules, business process flows, and calculated, rollup and formula columns.
    • Week 6: Run Focus Weak Spots every morning, then two end-to-end Exam Simulator runs at full 120-minute length. Schedule the exam when readiness gauge is 750+ with reasonable confidence.

Frequently asked

AB-410 FAQs

Is there a formal prerequisite for AB-410?

No formal prerequisite exam gates AB-410. Microsoft positions it as the successor to PL-200, and the PL-200T00 training course has been replaced by AB-410T00, but that's a training-path note rather than an exam gate — you can book AB-410 directly. If Power Platform concepts are genuinely new to you, PL-900 (Power Platform Fundamentals) is a sensible primer; makers already building apps and flows day to day can go straight in.

What does the AB-410 exam format actually look like?

Microsoft's general guidance for AB-410 is 40–60 questions, with the exact formats varying by sitting. In Azure Mastery's AB-410 bank, single-answer and scenario-based items carry most of the weight, alongside multi-select, drag-to-match, dropdown, hot-area, and case-study questions built as original practice content — not a copy of Microsoft's live item mix. Timed practice sessions run to the app's own session clock; adaptive study and Focus Weak Spots then target the domains where your accuracy is lowest.

AB-410 vs AB-620 — which Power Platform builder credential fits?

Both are Power Platform builder credentials, but they sit on opposite sides of the same solution. AB-410 builds the application — the Dataverse tables, the model-driven or canvas app, the cloud flow — that consumes an agent, a prompt, or an AI model. AB-620 builds that agent itself, inside Microsoft Copilot Studio: its topics, its knowledge sources, its extensions. A maker who owns a full solution end to end often ends up holding both.

Does AB-410 expect me to build the Copilot Studio agent itself?

No — building or extending the agent is AB-620 territory. AB-410 tests the maker side of that relationship: creating a canvas app that can spin up a Copilot Studio agent, consuming an existing agent or prompt inside a model-driven app, and wiring AI Hub prompts and AI Builder models into apps and flows. If a stem asks you to design the agent's conversation topics, that's the wrong exam for the question.

What does the AB-410 exam fee cover?

AB-410 costs USD $165 in the United States; Microsoft adjusts pricing by country and currency, so check the figure shown when you schedule through Microsoft Learn. That fee covers one proctored attempt. The Associate certification it unlocks then needs renewing every year, and that renewal is free.

I didn't pass AB-410 — how soon can I try again?

The first retake is available after 24 hours. A second or third attempt each requires a 14-day wait, and Microsoft caps you at five attempts in any rolling 12-month period. Every attempt, including retakes, needs its own voucher.

Is the AB-410 credential still valid after a year?

Yes. Associate certifications including AB-410 expire annually, and Microsoft renews them through a short, unproctored online assessment on Microsoft Learn, available during the six-month window before expiry. There's no fixed question count published for that assessment — it's shorter than the original exam, not a repeat of it. (Fundamentals certifications such as AZ-900 don't expire at all.)

How long should I spend preparing for AB-410?

Four to eight weeks of focused study is typical if you already build apps, flows, and Dataverse tables in Power Platform day to day. Give yourself the longer end of that range if Power Fx, prompt columns, or AI Hub are unfamiliar — the logic-and-automation domain alone carries close to half the exam. Azure Mastery's readiness gauge is a better signal than the calendar for when to actually book.
