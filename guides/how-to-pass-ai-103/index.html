How to pass AI-103: Developing AI Apps and Agents on Azure

A practical six-week route through Microsoft Foundry, agent development, and the vision, text, and retrieval domains — plus the topics that catch experienced Python developers off guard.

Updated 4 September 2026 · ~10 min read

The exam

What is AI-103, and is it for you?

AI-103 earns the Microsoft Certified: Azure AI Apps and Agents Developer Associate credential. It targets Azure AI engineers who build, manage, and deploy agents and generative AI solutions with Microsoft Foundry, and it is the exam most job adverts now mean when they ask for "Foundry experience" or "Azure AI application development" rather than a generic AI-adjacent skill set.

The exam expects practical Python experience and familiarity with general AI, generative AI, and Azure services. It replaced the retired AI-102 (Azure AI Engineer Associate) route, and the current fundamentals lead-in for candidates newer to the field is AI-901, Microsoft's live AI Fundamentals exam. AI-103 sits at the associate tier and is terminal there — it does not require a further exam, and none is required to sit it.

AI-103 became generally available on 1 June 2026, so it is still a young credential, which is exactly why solid preparation material is hard to find. It is hands-on and SDK-aware: you will be shown a Foundry project configuration, an agent workflow, or a retrieval pipeline and asked what it does or what to change, rather than asked to define a term. The skills measured were last confirmed on 16 April 2026, so anchor your study to that live outline rather than older AI-102 material, which covers a different, retired exam.

Format & domains

Exam format and skills domains

AI-103 mixes question styles rather than sticking to plain multiple choice. Expect single-answer and multi-select questions, ordering tasks around a pipeline or workflow, and short case studies that group several questions around one Foundry project scenario.

  • QuestionsRoughly 40–60
  • Duration100 minutes of exam time
  • Pass score700 / 1000 (scaled)
  • DeliveryOnline or a test centre
  • ValidityRenew free every year
  • FeeVaries by country

The exam fee changes from country to country, so check Microsoft's certification page for the price where you live. The five skills domains and their weights, from the official April 2026 outline, are below.

Six-week study plan

A realistic six-week study plan

Candidates who already build against Azure AI services or LLM APIs professionally tend to pass in five to eight weeks; if Foundry and agent orchestration are new to you, stretch this toward three months. Six weeks is the sensible middle path below, mapped onto the domains roughly in order of weight, with the largest domain split across two weeks.

  1. Plan and manage an Azure AI solution

    Choosing the right Foundry service for a generative or agentic task, including model families, grounding, vector search, and memory or tool integration. Then Azure infrastructure design for AI apps and agents, deployment options, and CI/CD integration for Foundry projects, followed by quotas, scaling, and monitoring, and responsible AI: guardrails, evaluators, trace logging, and oversight modes.

  2. Build generative applications with Foundry

    Deploy and consume large language models, small models, code models, and multimodal models through Foundry. Implement retrieval-augmented generation in an application, design tool-augmented and multistep reasoning workflows, and practise evaluating models and apps for fabrications, relevance, quality, and safety before you connect an app to a Foundry project with the SDKs.

  3. Build and optimise agents with Foundry

    Define agent roles, goals, and tool schemas, then build agents combining retrieval, function-calling, and conversation memory. Cover orchestrated multi-agent solutions and autonomous workflows with approval safeguards, then optimisation: prompt engineering, self-critique loops, and observability through tracing, token analytics, and latency breakdowns.

  4. Computer vision solutions

    Image- and video-generation from text prompts and reference media, plus inpainting and mask-based editing. Then multimodal understanding — captioning, visual question-answering, alt-text generation, and Azure Content Understanding pipelines for extracting visual characteristics. Close with responsible AI for visual content, including filters for unsafe imagery and indirect prompt injection through embedded image text.

  5. Text analysis and information extraction

    Language-model text analysis: entity, topic, and structured JSON extraction, sentiment and safety detection, and translation through Azure Translator or LLM-powered flows, plus speech-to-text and text-to-speech for agentic interactions. Then retrieval and grounding pipelines — semantic, hybrid, and vector search, RAG ingestion with OCR, and document extraction using Content Understanding analysers.

  6. Review and exam simulation

    Revisit responsible AI instrumentation and Foundry deployment detail across all five domains, since both thread through every section rather than sitting in one place. Then switch to sharpening: drill your weakest domains daily and sit two full-length timed practice runs. Book the exam once your readiness sits comfortably above the pass mark, not just at it.

Where people lose marks

The hardest AI-103 topics

One recurring theme cuts across all five domains: AI-103 rewards knowing which Foundry capability to reach for, not just what each one is called. These five areas consistently cost candidates marks.

Choosing a retrieval and grounding method

Semantic search, hybrid search, vector search, and plain keyword search all show up as options, and the exam expects you to match the method to the data shape and the accuracy requirement rather than default to the newest-sounding one. Practise reading a scenario and naming the exact retrieval approach it calls for, plus which Foundry service hosts it.

Multi-agent orchestration and safeguards

Questions test whether you can design an orchestrated multi-agent solution with the right balance of autonomy and approval control — when a workflow should run unattended, when it needs a human-in-the-loop checkpoint, and how tool-access constraints limit what an agent can do. The wording is subtle, so learn what each safeguard actually restricts.

Generation versus understanding in vision

The computer vision domain splits into generating new images and video and understanding existing ones, and it is easy to blur the two. Know which controls belong to image and video generation (inpainting, mask-based edits) versus multimodal understanding (captioning, visual question-answering, Content Understanding pipelines), because they solve different problems with different services.

Speech and translation service boundaries

Text analysis blends dedicated services with LLM-powered flows: Azure Translator versus prompt-based translation, and standard speech-to-text versus custom speech models for agentic interactions. Questions built around accuracy, latency, or customisation requirements expect you to pick the right one without defaulting to whichever you learned first.

Responsible AI instrumentation, not just policy

AI-103 tests the mechanics of responsible AI — evaluators, safety evaluations, explanation tooling, trace-based auditing, and provenance metadata — rather than the general principles. Expect a scenario that names a specific risk (fabrication, indirect prompt injection through an image, an ungrounded response) and asks which instrumentation catches it.

How to practise

Practise the way the exam works

Reading Foundry documentation builds recognition, not recall, and AI-103 tests recall of specific SDK and service behaviour under time pressure. Two habits close that gap directly. Retrieval practice: answer from memory before you check the explanation, because the effort of pulling a fact out of your own head is what fixes it, especially for the many "which Foundry service" decisions the exam is built on. Spaced repetition: come back to a domain a few days after you first study it rather than covering it once and moving on — the responsible-AI detail you nailed in week one is exactly what fades by week five if you never revisit it.

The third habit is hands-on repetition with the SDK itself. Deploy a small Foundry project, wire up a basic RAG flow, and build one simple agent with a single tool. Reading a code snippet becomes far faster once you have written a similar one yourself, and AI-103 leans heavily on exactly that skill. Finish with at least two full-length, mixed-domain simulator runs before you book, and review every wrong answer rather than just the score.

Exam day

Tactics for exam day

If you are sitting online, run the system check the day before and clear your desk, because proctors will ask you to show the room and remove anything within reach. Arrive early either way, since a rushed start eats into your focus during the dense scenario questions.

With 100 minutes for 40 to 60 questions you have roughly a minute and a half to two minutes each, which is comfortable if you keep moving. Use the mark-for-review flag on anything that makes you pause and return to it once you have banked the easy marks. Read Foundry SDK and pipeline configuration questions twice: the trap is usually a single parameter, service choice, or scope, and a slower second read pays off.

Case studies bundle several questions around one Foundry project scenario, and once you move past a case you generally cannot return to it, so answer everything inside a case fully before advancing. Remember the score is scaled: you need 700 out of 1000, not a perfect run, so an unfamiliar question is not a disaster. Flag it, make your best call, and keep your momentum.

Frequently asked

AI-103 FAQs

How hard is the AI-103 exam?

AI-103 is demanding because it assumes working Python experience and asks you to reason about Foundry SDK behaviour, not just recall service names. Expect scenarios that show you a model deployment, an agent workflow, or a retrieval pipeline and ask what to change or why it is behaving a certain way. Candidates who already build with Azure AI services or LLM APIs tend to pass after five to eight weeks of focused study; if generative AI development is new to you, allow longer.

How long should I study for AI-103?

Plan for five to eight weeks if you already write Python against AI or Azure services professionally. If Foundry, agent orchestration, and retrieval-augmented generation are new territory, two to three months is more realistic, because the exam rewards hands-on time with the SDKs over reading alone. The six-week plan on this page is a sensible middle path you can compress or stretch to fit your starting point.

Do I need AI-901 before AI-103?

AI-901 is not a formal prerequisite, but Microsoft's own certification path lists it as the current fundamentals route into AI-103. If you are new to AI concepts, responsible AI, and Microsoft Foundry at a conceptual level, sitting AI-901 first gives you vocabulary the associate exam assumes. If you already work with Python and Azure AI services day to day, you can go straight to AI-103.

What is the passing score for AI-103?

You need 700 out of 1000 to pass. The score is scaled rather than a raw percentage, so it does not map to a fixed number of correct answers, and harder questions carry more weight. Multi-select and ordering questions typically award no partial credit, so treat 700 as a comfortable target rather than a line to scrape over.

Does the AI-103 certification expire?

Yes. Like other Microsoft Associate certifications, AI-103 expires annually, and renewal is free. It is a short online assessment on Microsoft Learn that opens in the six-month window before your expiration date and targets recent updates to the skills outline, so staying current is manageable if you keep working with Foundry.

How much does AI-103 cost and can I retake it if I fail?

Exam pricing varies by country, so check Microsoft's certification page for the fee where you live rather than trusting a figure you read elsewhere. If you do not pass, the first retake is allowed after 24 hours, the second and third each require a 14-day wait, and Microsoft caps retakes at five attempts per rolling 12-month period. Each attempt needs a new booking.
