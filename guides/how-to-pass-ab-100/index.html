How to pass AB-100: Agentic AI Business Solutions Architect

A practical six-week route through planning, designing, and deploying agentic AI business solutions across Copilot Studio, Microsoft Foundry, and Dynamics 365 — plus the architecture trade-offs that catch experienced solution architects off guard.

Updated 4 September 2026 · ~10 min read

The exam

What is AB-100, and is it for you?

Passing AB-100 satisfies the exam requirement for the Microsoft Certified: Agentic AI Business Solutions Architect credential, Microsoft's expert-tier certification for architects who design end-to-end agentic AI business solutions across Microsoft Foundry, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Microsoft Copilot Studio, Dynamics 365, and the Power Platform. To earn the credential, Microsoft also requires one eligible associate certification; current options include AB-620 and AI-103. The exam is aimed at accomplished solution architects, not first-time AI candidates.

AB-100 sits at the top of Microsoft's agentic-AI stack. Where AB-620 validates building a single agent in Copilot Studio and AI-103 validates code-first Foundry development, AB-100 expects you to make cross-platform architecture calls — choosing between Microsoft 365 Copilot extensibility, Copilot Studio authoring, Microsoft Foundry custom code, and embedded Dynamics 365 Copilot — and to design the ALM, governance, security, and ROI story for the resulting estate. It is an expert-level exam and, like AZ-305 or AZ-400, is terminal at that tier: no further exam builds on it.

AB-100 became generally available on 1 May 2026, so prep material is still thin on the ground. The skills measured were last confirmed on 22 July 2026, so anchor your study to that live outline. Expect scenario-driven questions: a multi-paragraph business setup with a stated constraint, followed by a single architecture decision, rather than a definition to recall.

Format & domains

Exam format and skills domains

AB-100 mixes question styles: single-answer and multi-select items, ordering tasks around an ALM or governance process, and case studies that group several questions around one multi-platform business scenario.

Six-week study plan

A realistic six-week study plan

Use the six-week plan below as a starting point if you already architect solutions across Copilot Studio, Microsoft Foundry, and the Power Platform; extend it when one of those platforms is new to you. It tracks Plan and Design as single weeks each, then splits Deploy — the largest domain — across three weeks to match its weight and its four skill groups.

  1. Plan AI-powered business solutions

    Analyse requirements: where agents fit in task automation, analytics, and decision-making, and how to prepare data for grounding and reuse. Design the overall AI strategy: the Cloud Adoption Framework's AI adoption process, multi-agent solution design across Microsoft 365 Copilot, Copilot Studio, and Microsoft Foundry, when to build custom agents versus extend Microsoft 365 Copilot, and prompt-library and prompt-engineering guidelines. Close with evaluating costs and benefits: ROI and TCO criteria, build-versus-buy-versus-extend analysis, and model routing.

  2. Design AI and agents for business solutions

    Design Copilot customisations and connectors across Dynamics 365 apps for customer experience, service, and sales, and agents integrated with Contact Center channels. Learn the three agent archetypes — task, autonomous, and prompt-and-response — plus designing Copilot Studio topics with fallback, AI components in a Power Apps canvas app, and when to reach for standard natural language processing, conversational language understanding, or generative AI orchestration.

  3. Design extensibility, and orchestrate prebuilt agents

    Design AI solutions with custom Foundry models, agents in Microsoft 365 Copilot, and agent extensibility in Copilot Studio through the Model Context Protocol and Computer Use. Then orchestrate prebuilt agents and apps: AI features in Dynamics 365 finance, supply chain, and customer-experience apps, configuring Microsoft 365 Copilot for Sales and Service, and adding knowledge sources to in-app help and guidance.

  4. Analyse, monitor, and test AI-powered solutions

    Recommend the process and tooling to monitor agents, analyse feedback and backlog, and interpret telemetry for tuning. Then testing: validation criteria for custom models, Copilot prompt best-practice checks, and end-to-end test scenarios spanning multiple Dynamics 365 apps.

  5. ALM, responsible AI, security, and governance

    Design the ALM process across four different surfaces — Copilot Studio agents and connectors, the Foundry Agents service, custom AI models, and AI inside Dynamics 365 apps. Then design security for agents and models, analyse vulnerabilities including prompt manipulation, validate data residency and access controls on grounding data, and design audit trails for model and data changes.

  6. Review and exam simulation

    Revisit the trade-off pattern that runs through every domain: distractors that are correct under a different constraint. Then switch to sharpening: drill your weakest domain daily and sit two full-length timed practice runs. Book the exam once your readiness sits comfortably above the pass mark, not just at it.

Focus areas

The hardest AB-100 topics

AB-100 rewards architecture judgement over recall. These five areas deserve focused practice because the "correct" answer changes with the stated constraint.

Picking the right platform boundary

Microsoft 365 Copilot extensibility, Copilot Studio authoring, Microsoft Foundry custom code, and embedded Dynamics 365 Copilot all overlap in capability, and the exam frequently tests which one is the primary surface for a given task. Practise reading a requirement and naming the platform that owns it, not just a platform that could do it.

Trade-off questions, not definition questions

Most AB-100 distractors are correct under a different constraint — cost versus latency, data residency, governance maturity, build-versus-buy. Learn to isolate the stated constraint in the scenario before you evaluate the options, because two answers can both be technically sound and only one fits the requirement as written.

ALM across four different agent surfaces

Copilot Studio agents and connectors, the Foundry Agents service, custom AI models, and Dynamics 365 apps each need a distinct application-lifecycle design, and the exam expects you to know which promotion pattern, environment strategy, and versioning approach belongs to which surface rather than one generic ALM answer.

Responsible AI, security, and governance as one skill

AB-100 blends model security, prompt-manipulation mitigation, data residency, access controls on grounding data, and audit trails into a single skill group, and treats them as design decisions rather than a compliance checklist. Expect a scenario that names a specific risk and asks which control addresses it, not a general principle to restate.

ROI, TCO, and the build-buy-extend call

The Plan domain's cost-and-benefit skill group is easy to underrate, but architect candidates are expected to select ROI criteria, build a TCO-aware business case, and judge when to build, buy, or extend an AI component rather than defaulting to a custom solution because it is the most interesting option.

How to practise

Practise the way the exam works

Reading Microsoft's architecture documentation builds vocabulary, but AB-100 tests judgement calls under a stated business constraint, which is a different skill entirely. Scenario practice closes that gap: read the full business setup before you look at the question, identify the constraint that rules out an otherwise sensible answer, and only then commit to your choice. Doing this from memory, rather than recognising the right answer among four options, is what builds the instinct the real exam rewards.

Pair that with spaced repetition across all three domains, since Design and Deploy in particular hold enough distinct skill groups that any one of them can fade if you study it once and move on. Then close with full-length simulation: a timed, mixed-domain run is the only way to check whether you can keep spotting constraints in the back half of the paper, once fatigue sets in. Sit at least two before you book, and review every wrong answer rather than just the score.

Exam day

Tactics for exam day

If you are sitting online, run the system check the day before and clear your desk, since proctors will ask you to show the room and remove anything within reach. Arrive early either way, because the scenario questions reward a settled, unhurried read rather than a rushed skim.

With 100 minutes for 40 to 60 questions you have roughly a minute and a half to two minutes each. Use the mark-for-review flag on anything that makes you pause and return to it once you have banked the easy marks. Read every business scenario for the stated constraint before you look at the options — AB-100 questions routinely bury the deciding detail in a subordinate clause about cost, residency, or governance rather than the headline requirement.

Case studies bundle several questions around one multi-platform business scenario, and once you move past a case you generally cannot return to it, so answer everything inside a case fully before advancing. Remember the score is scaled: you need 700 out of 1000, not a perfect run, so an unfamiliar scenario is not a disaster. Flag it, make your best call, and keep moving.

Frequently asked

AB-100 FAQs

How hard is the AB-100 exam?

AB-100 is an architecture-decision exam, not a name-the-feature exam, and that makes it harder to cram for than a typical associate certification. Most questions describe a business scenario with a stated constraint — cost, data residency, governance maturity, build-versus-buy — and ask you to pick the design that fits that constraint, not just a design that would technically work. If you are newer to Copilot Studio, Microsoft Foundry, or Dynamics 365, budget extra hands-on time before relying on practice results.

How long should I study for AB-100?

The six-week plan on this page is a practical starting point if you already design solutions spanning Copilot Studio, Microsoft Foundry, and the Power Platform professionally. Extend it when any of those platforms is new territory, because the exam expects working familiarity across all of them rather than depth in just one. Use your domain-level practice results to decide when to add or repeat a week.

Do I need AB-620 or AI-103 before AB-100?

You need one eligible associate certification as well as AB-100 to earn the expert credential, but Microsoft does not require AB-620 or AI-103 specifically. Both are accepted options alongside several Dynamics 365 and Power Platform associate certifications. AI-901 is a fundamentals credential and does not satisfy this associate-level requirement. AB-100 tests architecture judgement across platforms, so choose the associate route that best matches your hands-on work.

Is AB-100 the same as AB-620 or AI-103?

No. AB-620 validates building a single agent inside Microsoft Copilot Studio, and AI-103 validates developing AI apps and agents in code against Microsoft Foundry. AB-100 sits above both: it expects you to architect multi-agent solutions that span Copilot Studio, Microsoft Foundry, Microsoft 365 Copilot, and Dynamics 365 apps, and to design the ALM, governance, security, and ROI story for the resulting estate. Take the implementation-level exam that matches your day-to-day role first; move to AB-100 once you are making cross-platform architecture calls.

What is the passing score for AB-100, and does it expire?

You need 700 out of 1000 to pass, on a scaled score rather than a raw percentage, so it does not map to a fixed number of correct answers. Like Microsoft's other certifications, AB-100 also expires annually, with a free online renewal assessment on Microsoft Learn that opens in the six-month window before your expiration date.

How much does AB-100 cost and can I retake it if I fail?

Exam pricing varies by country, so check Microsoft's certification page for the fee where you live rather than trusting a figure you read elsewhere. If you do not pass, the first retake is allowed after 24 hours, the second and third each require a 14-day wait, and Microsoft caps retakes at five attempts per rolling 12-month period. Each attempt needs a new booking.
