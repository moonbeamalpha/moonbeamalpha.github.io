The exam

What is the AZ-900 exam?

AZ-900 is the foundational Azure certification — the standard starting point for cloud careers in the Microsoft ecosystem. It's accessible enough for sales engineers, project managers, and finance partners, but rigorous enough that a meaningful share of candidates fail their first attempt.

The exam validates that you can describe core Azure services, recognise cloud computing concepts (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS), and explain Azure architecture, governance, identity, and security.

AZ-900 is intentionally non-technical at the surface — it doesn't expect you to write PowerShell scripts or design network topologies. What it does expect is a clear conceptual map: regions, availability zones, resource groups, the shared responsibility model, Azure Policy, and the defence-in-depth security model — all explained in terms a non-engineer can defend in a meeting.

Common traps

Where AZ-900 candidates slip

Five recurring misconceptions that trip up otherwise well-prepared AZ-900 candidates, grounded in the current skills outline.

IaaS vs PaaS vs SaaS: responsibility decides the answer

Candidates memorise the three definitions without the responsibility trade-off that actually decides the answer.

  • OS patching and other named layers each sit with either you or Microsoft, depending on the service type in the scenario.
  • Match the scenario's described responsibility split to IaaS, PaaS, or SaaS rather than recalling a definition.

Availability zones vs region pairs

Candidates confuse surviving one datacentre failure with where data replicates during a regional outage.

  • Zones protect against a single datacentre failure within one region; region pairs protect against losing the whole region.
  • Match "one datacentre goes down" to zones and "regional outage replication" to region pairs.

CapEx vs OpEx under the consumption model

Candidates try to calculate a bill instead of recognising the CapEx-to-OpEx shift a scenario is testing.

  • Retiring on-premises hardware for cloud consumption is the classic CapEx-to-OpEx scenario the exam keeps returning to.
  • Read for a shift from buying hardware upfront to paying for use — that's the answer, not a cost figure.

Azure Policy vs RBAC

Candidates can't tell a configuration rule from an access-control rule in the same governance scenario.

  • Policy enforces what a configuration is allowed to look like; RBAC controls who is allowed to act at all.
  • Match "blocking a configuration" to Policy and "restricting who can create resources" to RBAC.

Zero Trust is a principle, not a product

Candidates look for a single "Zero Trust service" instead of recognising a security philosophy in the scenario.

  • Zero Trust is a security philosophy Microsoft applies across its whole stack, not a single purchasable product or service.
  • Look for verify-explicitly, least-privilege, or assume-breach language as the cue, not a service name.

Frequently asked

AZ-900 FAQs

How much does the AZ-900 exam cost?

The AZ-900 voucher is USD $99 in the United States. Pricing varies by region — in the UK it's typically around £77. Microsoft sometimes runs free-voucher promotions for events such as Microsoft Build or Microsoft Ignite, so check your Microsoft Learn profile for any active offers before booking.

Does the AZ-900 certification expire?

No. Microsoft Fundamentals certifications — including AZ-900, DP-900, AI-901, MS-900, and PL-900 — do not expire. Once you pass, the certification is yours for life. (This is different from Associate and Expert certifications such as AZ-104 or AZ-305, which require an annual free renewal assessment on Microsoft Learn.)

What is the AZ-900 retake policy if I fail?

Retake waits get longer the more times you've failed, and every attempt needs its own voucher.

  • First retake: after 24 hours.
  • Second and third retakes: a 14-day wait each.
  • Cap: five attempts per rolling 12 months.
How long should I study for AZ-900?

Most candidates with prior IT experience pass after one to two weeks of focused revision. If cloud computing is genuinely new to you, plan for three to four weeks. Azure Mastery's readiness gauge will tell you when you're at exam-ready — don't book until it shows roughly 720 or higher predicted score with reasonable confidence.

AZ-900 vs AZ-104 — which should I take first?

AZ-900 first, almost always. AZ-900 is designed as the on-ramp to AZ-104 (Microsoft Azure Administrator). It takes a few weeks to prepare for and doesn't expire; AZ-104 takes two to three months and requires hands-on PowerShell and CLI knowledge. Most people pass AZ-900 to confirm cloud is the right direction, then commit to AZ-104.

Is AZ-900 worth taking if I'm not technical?

Yes — that's exactly who it's designed for. Account managers, sales engineers, and finance partners sit AZ-900 to qualify for Microsoft Partner Network roles. It doesn't expect a PowerShell script, just an ability to explain region pairs, shared responsibility, and when hybrid cloud fits.

What prerequisites does AZ-900 assume?

AZ-900 has no formal prerequisites — Microsoft designed it as the common entry point into Azure. Some IT background makes the material quicker to absorb, since you already have an environment to map its concepts onto. Coming from a fully non-technical background stretches the usual one-to-two-week estimate.
